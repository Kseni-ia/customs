## 3. Why these codes — the reasoning

### Step 1: Chapter 95 (fishing tackle) is ruled out

The obvious instinct is heading **9507** ("fishing rods, fish-hooks and **other line fishing tackle**") — it is
cheaper (3.7 %) and the products are unmistakably fishing goods. It does not apply here.

The Explanatory Notes to heading 95.07 expressly exclude:

> *"Yarns, monofilament, cords or gut or the like for fishing, cut to length but **not made up into fishing
> lines**, of Chapter 39, heading 42.06 or Section XI."*

"Made up into a fishing line" in HS terms means the line is **assembled into finished tackle** — fitted with
hooks, swivels, floats, leaders, i.e. a ready-to-fish rig. Line simply wound onto a spool, however
branded and retail-packed, is still bulk line and stays in **Section XI (textiles)**.

Nothing in this consignment is rigged: every one of the 19 articles is plain line on a spool.

### Step 2: Monofilament → heading 5404

Heading **5404** covers *"synthetic monofilament of 67 decitex or more and of which no cross-sectional
dimension exceeds 1 mm"*. Both conditions are met on every mono line here:

- **Diameter:** 0.25 – 0.95 mm → all under the 1 mm ceiling.
- **Linear density (calculated):** nylon 0.33 mm ≈ 975 dtex; fluorocarbon 0.25 mm ≈ 874 dtex up to
  0.95 mm ≈ 12,600 dtex → all far above the 67 dtex floor.

Subheading: 5404 11 = elastomeric, 5404 12 = polypropylene, **5404 19 = other** → nylon (polyamide) and
fluorocarbon (PVDF) both land in **5404 19 00**. Same code either way, so the fluorocarbon/nylon
distinction carries no duty risk.

### Step 3: Braided line → heading 5607

Heading **5607** covers *"twine, cordage, ropes and cables, **whether or not plaited or braided**"*.

The relevant test for a braid is the one in the Explanatory Notes to 56.07: braids of this heading are
**tubular, tightly plaited, of compact structure and suitable for use as twine/cordage** — as opposed to
the loose decorative braids of heading 5808. An 8-strand (X8 / X8+1) hollow-braided gel-spun PE fishing
line is the textbook example of a tight, compact, tubular braid.

Subheading:
- **5607 49** = of polyethylene or polypropylene → our PE braids.
- 5607 50 = of other synthetic fibres (nylon, polyester).
- Within 5607 49, the split is at 50,000 dtex. Calculated linear density of these braids is roughly
  **230 – 1,050 dtex** (0.19 mm to 0.40 mm), i.e. nowhere near the threshold → **5607 49 90**
  ("measuring 50,000 decitex (5 g/m) or less").

This matches settled practice elsewhere (US CBP rulings NY 882320 and NY 881149 both put gel-spun
polyethylene and other braided fishing line in 5607.49/5607.50, and monofilament ≤1 mm in 5404).

## 4. Alternatives considered and rejected

| Code | Duty | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| **9507 90 00** — other line fishing tackle | 3.7 % | Excluded by the EN to 95.07 — line not made up into finished fishing lines (no hooks/rigs). **This is the main contestable point** — see risk note below. |
| **5808 10 00** — braids in the piece | 5.0 % | Reserved for loose/ornamental braids; our braid is tight and compact, cordage-like → 5607. |
| **5607 50 90** — braid of other synthetic fibres | 8.0 % | Correct code **only if** a braid turns out not to be chiefly polyethylene by weight. Same duty rate, so no financial exposure — but the code would be wrong. Relevant to art. RC0058 (see Q1). |
| **3916 90 90** — plastic monofilament >1 mm | 6.5 % | Only if actual diameter exceeds 1.00 mm. The 0.95 mm fluorocarbon is close to that line — see risk note. |
| **5604 90 90** — yarn coated with plastics | 4.0 % | Only if a "fluorocarbon" line is actually nylon **coated** with PVDF rather than solid PVDF. Same duty rate. |

### Risk notes

1. **9507 vs Section XI is the one genuinely arguable point.** My reading (Section XI) is the
   conservative, well-supported one and costs more duty — 8 % vs 3.7 % on the braid. If Sonik has
   previously imported the same lines into the EU under 9507 90 00, or holds a BTI from another member
   state, tell me: consistency across declarations matters more than my preference, and a conflicting
   history needs to be reconciled before we file.
2. **The 0.95 mm fluorocarbon (art. 30600027).** Labelled diameters on fishing line are nominal and real
   diameter is often over. If it measures **≥ 1.00 mm**, that article alone moves out of 5404 into
   **3916 90 90 at 6.5 %**. Worth a caliper check on a sample, or a written diameter confirmation from
   Dah Sen.
3. **Given this is a repeat supplier, a Czech ZTI (závazná informace o sazebním zařazení) is worth
   filing** — one for the braid, one for the mono. It removes the 9507 argument permanently and protects
   against a retrospective assessment on future shipments.

## 5. ⚠️ Preferential origin — NO `preferenční věta`, standard duty applies

I checked the invoice, the packing list and the B/L. **There is no preferential-origin declaration
anywhere in the documents** — only the plain statement *"Country of The Origin: CHINA"*.

That is the expected outcome and it is correct:

- **The EU has no free-trade agreement with China**, and China has been graduated out of GSP since 2015.
  There is no preferential regime to invoke.
- **Full third-country (erga omnes) duty applies: 8.0 % on the braid, 4.0 % on the monofilament.**

> **Do not let the GB leg create a false preference claim.** The invoice comes from SONIK SPORTS LIMITED
> in Great Britain and the goods are transhipped in Felixstowe, so it may be tempting to reach for the
> EU–UK TCA and declare 0 %. **That would be wrong.** The goods are of Chinese origin and undergo no
> processing in the UK — mere transhipment/repacking confers nothing, and the TCA is unavailable. A
> preference claim here would be a straightforward post-clearance recovery plus penalty. If anyone on
> the Sonik side sends a UK statement on origin, it must be refused.

**Other measures checked in TARIC (reference date 11.08.2026, origin CN) for 5607 49 90, 5607 50 90,
5404 19 00 and 9507 90 00:**

- **Anti-dumping / countervailing duty: none.** No measure in force on any of these codes against China.
- **CBAM: not applicable** — no iron/steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser, electricity or hydrogen.
- No import licence, quota, or surveillance measure applies.
- Import VAT 21 % in CZ as normal.

## 6. Customs value and other points to settle before the JSD

1. **Freight is COLLECT — it is not in the USD 27,090.09.** Under Art. 71 UCC the customs value must
   include sea freight, THC/handling and insurance **up to the point where the goods enter the EU
   customs territory**. Because the goods land in Felixstowe (third country post-Brexit) and continue by
   road, the addable costs run all the way to the EU border crossing, not just to Felixstowe. Costs from
   the EU border onwards to Osov are deductible **only if separately shown**. We need the freight
   invoice(s) split accordingly.
2. **Which sale is the customs value based on?** The invoice we hold is China → **SONIK SPORTS LIMITED
   (GB)**, but the importer/consignee is **SONIK SPORTS CZECH LOGISTICS (CZ)**. Under Art. 128 UCC IA
   the transaction value must be the sale **immediately before the goods were brought into the EU**. If
   SONIK GB re-sells to SONIK CZ, that GB→CZ invoice is the one that governs — and we do not have it.
3. **Related parties.** SONIK GB and SONIK CZ appear to be the same group. If the GB→CZ invoice is used,
   Art. 70(3)(d) UCC requires that the relationship did not influence the price; box 8.3 of the DV1 and,
   if asked, a transfer-pricing/comparable-price justification.
4. **USD → CZK** at the monthly customs exchange rate valid on the date of acceptance of the declaration.
5. **Repack in GB changes the packaging data.** The 57 cartons become 2–3 pallets. The gross weight on
   the JSD must reflect the actual palletised weight (pallet tare included), not the 501.90 kg from the
   B/L. Vladimír to confirm the final pallet count and weight.
6. **Net weight includes the plastic spools** as declared on the packing list. If the customs office
   queries net mass for the textile headings, the spool weight may need stripping out — worth flagging
   only if challenged.
7. **No trade discount** appears on the invoice, so no proportional discount allocation is needed.
8. Minor discrepancy for the record: B/L 2.647 CBM vs packing list 2.51 CBM. Weights agree (501.90 kg).
